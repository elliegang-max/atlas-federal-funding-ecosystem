Atlas

A map of the federal funding ecosystem for university spinouts

Describe your research in plain English, get the federal funding programmes that actually fit — ranked, sourced, with a contact attached.

Ellie Gang · built for Yale Ventures · October 2026

The problem

A discovery leaves the grant-funded world long before it is ready for venture capital. The bridge is non-dilutive federal money — SBIR, STTR, and the translational programmes beside them.

Yale Ventures' job is to walk spinouts across that bridge. To do it they need to know which programmes exist, what each funds, at what stage, and who to call.

There is no aggregated map of this ecosystem built for early-stage research spinouts. Grants.gov and SBIR.gov list opportunities; neither explains how the agencies relate.

Before Atlas it was purely word of mouth. Our artefact was one spreadsheet with ~20 people — most with function and title unknown. Building a usable picture took a couple of months. — Yale Ventures leadership (Morag Grassie), Oct 2026
30–100 federal funding bodies are in scope. SBIR + STTR alone obligate $4.7B/yr across 11 agencies (SBA FY22).

What is new — honestly

AI grant matching is not new. Granted AI, Scout, Grantify and TurboInnovate all do it. Scout even targets tech transfer offices, free.

What existsWhat it misses
Granted AI — matching + network graphs Foundation-centric; graphs show money flow, not an agency's structure. No contacts.
Scout — free matching, names TTOs Organised around the solicitation. No contact layer, no map.
Grants.gov / SBIR.gov — authoritative Assume you know what to search. SBIR.gov misses ARPA-H, BARDA, CDMRP, DIU, In-Q-Tel.

The gap: search for a federal funding ecosystem map built for technology transfer and you get spending dashboards. No product serves this user.

Atlas is the layer underneath the matching: organizations and their relationships — not opportunities — scoped to venture arms, with a sourced contact layer, and two interfaces over one graph.

How the agents work

Three model agents. The two cheapest stages — the Guard and Retrieval — are plain Python, and that is why a search costs seven cents. The map calls no model at all.

Interface 1 — the ecosystem map

118 organizations, 101 with a venture arm, placed by hand so the model can never invent an agency.

Click any node for its overview, check sizes, application timeline, eligibility, contact point — and how it relates to its parent.

NINR sits inside NIH and shares its SBIR mechanism. No amount of browsing nih.gov makes that obvious. That relationship is the thing the map exists to show.

Interface 2 — find my funding

"A gene therapy targeting TYK2 to slow multiple sclerosis. Mouse data, no company yet. $2–3M over two years."

NINDS ranked 1st — and the agent flagged that NINDS's Blueprint network funds small molecules, not gene therapy, and that Yale itself cannot be the applicant.

Every match carries its sources, a confidence rating, and the honest reasons it might not work. 45 seconds, $0.07.

Safety: accuracy is the risk

A wrong contact is only discovered after the outreach is sent. So the defenses are ordered by damage.

The audit caught a real defect: navy-sbir-sttr.com, a lookalike domain matching no real organization. A provenance filter now strips any citation that is not federal or curated.

Injection blocked before any model call — cost $0.00. Spend ceiling $5.00/process, 20 requests/5 min/IP.

Next: verify every citation at build time
